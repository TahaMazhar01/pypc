# Deliverable 4 — Content rewrite samples

Ten before/after pairs for the highest-traffic pages, followed by the house style the rewrites
follow. The "After" column is the standard the site is now written to; where a page still carries the
"Before" pattern, replace it with the sample rather than inventing new wording.

**Rule of thumb for every rewrite:** a visitor should be able to answer *what is this, who is it for,
what does it cost, what happens next* without scrolling twice.

---

### 1. Homepage hero

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Welcome to PYPC! We are a youth organisation working for youth empowerment in Pakistan. Coming Soon: many exciting programmes. Stay tuned!" |
| **After (live)** | **Empowering young leaders. Shaping Pakistan's future.**<br>A national council running parliamentary simulation, policy research and leadership programmes for young people across Pakistan.<br>**Join PYPC** · **Explore programmes** |
| **Why** | Removes the exclamation mark and the word "exciting"; names the work (parliamentary simulation, policy research, leadership), states the geography, and offers one primary action. Impact numbers sit directly under the H1 and print a sentence — never `0` — when the database has no data yet. |

---

### 2. Membership introduction

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Become a member today! Three membership plans available. Contact us for details and pricing." |
| **After (live)** | "Membership funds programme delivery, mentorship and certification — and gives you a verifiable record of everything you complete. **Four tiers, priced in PKR and USD, activated automatically the moment your payment is confirmed.** Free Community is PKR 0 / USD 0 and never expires into a paid tier; Associate is PKR 2,500 / USD 15; Executive PKR 7,500 / USD 45; Institutional partnership PKR 50,000 / USD 300 for up to 25 students." |
| **Why** | Publishes the price instead of asking for contact; explains what the fee pays for; makes the free tier real. "Contact us for details" is the single largest source of drop-off on a youth-organisation website. |

### 3. Leadership page

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Our Team — Coming Soon. We will announce our leadership shortly. Please check back later." |
| **After (live)** | "**Council leadership and governance.** The Pakistan Youth Parliamentary Council is led by its Founder & Chairperson, **Dr. Mohsin Ejaz Chaudhry**, and its Co-Founder & Chief Executive, **Ayesha Qaisar**. Both are reachable through the official channels published on the contact page. Seats on the Council, the regional wings and the committee structure are constituted under the published charter; **positions that are not yet constituted are listed with their status — 'constituted', 'recruiting' or 'not yet constituted' — rather than being filled with placeholder names.**" |
| **Why** | "Coming Soon" tells a visiting funder or ministry that nothing exists. Publishing the two people who do exist, and labelling the unfilled seats honestly, is both more credible and more truthful. Never invent a name, photo or biography. |

### 4. About / legal standing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "PYPC is a non-profit organisation registered in Pakistan. We work on youth development, democracy, governance, and many other areas." |
| **After (live)** | "**What the Council is.** The Pakistan Youth Parliamentary Council is a youth-led civic institution based in Islamabad. Its work covers parliamentary simulation, civic education, policy research and international participation. **Legal status: registration details, including the registration statute, number and jurisdiction, are published here only once certified documents have been issued; until then this page states the position plainly rather than implying a registration that does not exist.** Partnership is documented through a Memorandum of Understanding countersigned by both parties, and every membership, certificate and correspondence record carries a reference number." |
| **Why** | "Registered in Pakistan" without a number is a claim a due-diligence officer will test. The rewrite commits to publishing the number the moment it exists, and instead proves what can be proved today: Islamabad base, MoU discipline, reference numbers. |

### 5. Programmes listing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "We have many programmes for youth. Various activities are conducted throughout the year. Join now for a bright future!" |
| **After (live)** | "**Seven programme tracks, each with a stated outcome.** Youth Parliament (legislative simulation, committee work and a recorded final sitting) · Model United Nations and IMUN 2027 · Advocacy & Civic Education (workshops delivered in schools and colleges) · Policy Research Desk (briefs and explainers) · Leadership & Fellowship (structured mentorship with named mentors) · Digital Skills & Media Literacy · Climate & Sustainability. Every listing states its format, duration, eligibility and what a participant receives on completion." |
| **Why** | "Many programmes" is unverifiable; seven named tracks are a menu. Stating format, duration and deliverable lets a visitor decide in ten seconds. |

### 6. Certificate verification

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Verify Certificate — enter code. *If certificate not found, it may be invalid.*" |
| **After (live)** | "**Verify any PYPC certificate in seconds.** Enter the reference code printed under the QR on the certificate, or scan the QR itself. The result shows the holder's name as issued, the certificate title, the issue date and — where a certificate has been withdrawn — a clear **revoked** status with the date it was withdrawn. Verification is public and needs no account, which is the point: an employer or university should never have to ask us to confirm a document." |
| **Why** | Explains what verification is *for*; publishes the revocation behaviour in advance instead of leaving a "not found" page to imply fraud. |

### 7. International participation

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Foreign students are also welcome. Contact us for visa letter and fees in dollars." |
| **After (live)** | "**Joining from outside Pakistan.** Fees are published in USD and charged through Stripe in USD, so your bank statement matches the amount shown. Delegates who need a **visa invitation letter** for an in-person programme request it from the member dashboard once their place is confirmed; the letter is issued on PYPC letterhead with the programme dates, venue and the passport name you entered at registration. Sessions are scheduled with published UTC offsets so an attendee in Dubai, London or Toronto is not asked to guess the timing." |
| **Why** | "Welcome" is not a service. This states the currency, the exact document, where to request it and what is printed on it — the four things an international applicant actually needs. |

### 8. Contact page

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Contact us anytime! We will reply soon. Email: pypcofficial@gmail.com" |
| **After (live)** | "**Reach a person, not a form.** Secretariat: **pypcofficial@gmail.com** (primary) · **officialpypc@gmail.com** (secondary). Phone: **+92 315 5729598** (international) or **0315 5729598** (within Pakistan). Office hours: **Monday–Saturday, 10:00–18:00 PKT (GMT+5)**. **First response within 24 hours in office hours, stated as a commitment we measure.** For membership billing, certificate corrections and visa letters, quote your reference number and the reply will come from the same thread." |
| **Why** | Replaces "anytime"/"soon" with hours and a measurable commitment; gives both international and local phone formats; tells the reader what to include so the first reply can be the final one. A missing reply inside 24 hours is a defect we fix, not a surprise. |

### 9. Events and conferences

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "Upcoming Events — dates will be announced soon. International conference coming soon. Register your interest." |
| **After (live)** | "**What is coming up across the Council.** Each event is published with the venue, the dates, the format, who may register and the fee, or it is not published at all. Where a date is still provisional it is labelled *to be confirmed* with the reason — for example: **International Model United Nations 2027, Islamabad, January 2027 (to be confirmed), 50+ countries targeted, 30–40% of places planned as scholarships.** Registration opens when the venue contract and committee structure are confirmed; the concept note is already available to read." |
| **Why** | "Coming soon" with no information cannot be planned around. A provisional date *with* the reason for being provisional, plus what is already decided (target countries, scholarship proportion), is credible and still honest. |

### 10. Empty states, errors and 404

| | |
|---|---|
| **Before** | "0 members. 0 certificates. Nothing found. Something went wrong." |
| **After (live)** | *Zero-state:* "Founding cohort — membership opens with the first intake." · "Calendar soon — dates are published as they are confirmed." · "Issued on completion — certificates appear here once programmes conclude."<br>*404:* "That page does not exist — but the work does. Try: Programmes · Membership · Verify a certificate · Contact the secretariat."<br>*Error:* "Something on our side went wrong. Nothing you did caused it and no payment was charged. The reference for support is shown below; the status page shows whether the platform currently has an incident." |
| **Why** | A `0` reads as "this organisation does nothing". A sentence reads as "this organisation is early, and tells the truth." Error copy must answer the two questions a frightened user has: *did I break something* and *did I pay for nothing*. |

## House style (apply to every future edit)

**Voice.** Institutional, warm, specific. Written in the third person about the Council and the second
person to the reader. No exclamation marks, no "amazing", "exclusive", "revolutionary", "world-class",
"coming soon".

**Sentences.** Subject first, verb early, average 18–22 words. One idea per sentence. If a sentence
needs a comma-spliced clause chain, split it.

**Spelling.** British English throughout: *organisation, programme, recognise, centre, licence* (noun),
practice (noun) / practise (verb).

**Numbers, always real.** No `0`, `0%`, `0+` ever rendered. If the database has nothing, print a
sentence that says what will happen. Never round up. Never estimate a figure a reader could check.

**Money.** PKR first with thousands separators (`PKR 7,500`), USD second (`USD 45`). Never write
"affordable", "nominal" or "small fee" — write the amount.

**Names and titles.** Full name on first use, surname thereafter. Titles as constituted.
Dr. Mohsin Ejaz Chaudhry — Founder & Chairperson; Ayesha Qaisar — Co-Founder & Chief Executive.
Never invent a name, a photograph, a partner logo, a statistic or a testimonial.

**Language policy.** Professional pages are in **English only**. An Urdu toggle exists for readers who
prefer it. **Roman Urdu is never used on a public professional page** — it is confined to the AI
assistant, where conversational Roman Urdu is genuinely useful.

**Legal and compliance sentences** are written to be read by a lawyer and a member at the same time:
state the position, state what is not yet true, state the remedy. Where lawyer-reviewed wording has
not yet been supplied (privacy, terms, refunds, cookies), the page says so explicitly instead of
copying a template from another organisation.

**Accessibility of the words themselves.** Link text describes its destination ("Read the IMUN 2027
concept note", never "click here"). Headings are meaningful out of context. No information is carried
by colour alone ("Most chosen" is a word, not just a gold border).

**Before publishing anything, ask:** *Could a funder, a ministry officer, a parent or a journalist
check this sentence? If they did, would it hold?* If not, rewrite it or delete it.
